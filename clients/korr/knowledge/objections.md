# KORR Medical Technologies — Objections

_Source: `KORR_Reddit_Skill.pdf`, provided directly by user, 2026-08-07._

## "My watch already tells me my VO2 max — why would I need a lab test?"

**Factual response:** wearables estimate VO2 max from heart-rate/pace models; KORR
measures it directly via breath-by-breath gas exchange. The two numbers can legitimately
differ because one measures and one estimates — this isn't a defect in either, it's a
methodology difference.

## "Why don't my watch and lab numbers match?"

**Factual response:** small training changes, heat, stress, or sleep can shift a
wearable's model-based estimate without a real underlying change; a direct measurement
isn't subject to the same drift. Protocol quality also matters — a bad ramp-rate protocol
produces misleading lab numbers too, on either method.

## "Is VO2 max worth measuring at all if I'm not racing?"

**Factual response (from the source material's example):** higher VO2 max correlates
with lower all-cause mortality at a population level — it's more a health/longevity
marker than a pure performance metric. That said, VO2 max represents aerobic capacity
(the ceiling), not race performance — economy, thresholds, and durability matter as much
or more.

## Hard rules

- Never claim lab testing "always" beats wearable estimates — the source material
  explicitly models uncertainty here ("I'm not sure how much it matters for training
  decisions vs. just being an interesting data point").
- Never use fear-based or absolutist framing ("you must get tested," "wearables are
  useless").
