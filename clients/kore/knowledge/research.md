## The 10 Gold Standard Pieces (type -> what to emulate)

1. **Core Connected Health narrative** ("Connectivity: The New Vital Sign in Remote
   Patient Care") — lead with a human, clinical framing before technology; use the
   connectivity-as-infrastructure angle, not a features angle.
2. **Operational/OEM audience piece** ("Why Connectivity Failures Are the Silent Threat
   in Healthcare IoT") — open with the operational consequence, not the technology; name
   specific failure modes, not vague "connectivity issues."
3. **SEO pillar / foundational explainer** ("What Is Cellular IoT?") — clear H2 structure
   mirroring search intent; define every term; KORE as the guide, not the subject.
4. **Annual trend / thought leadership** ("IoT Insights & Trends: What 2025 Taught Us
   and What Is Ahead in 2026") — reference specific industry developments, not vague
   trends; balance optimism with realism.
5. **Technical comparison / decision support** ("APN vs VPN") — use "X vs Y" with a
   genuine answer, not "it depends"; create a decision framework; end with actionable
   next steps.
6. **Emerging tech comparison** ("iSIM vs vSIM") — acknowledge genuine uncertainty; don't
   editorialize, let the comparison do the work.
7. **Event / brand narrative** ("KORE at CES 2026") — use specific names and moments, not
   generalities; the one appropriate exception to the formal register (conversational is
   fine here).
8. **High-authority foundational explainer** ("What Is an eSIM?") — answer the title
   question in the first two sentences; every paragraph independently comprehensible.
9. **Systems-level vertical storytelling** ("Inside Virtual Wards") — "follow the data"
   narrative structure: start at the patient, end at the clinician; technology is the
   means, not the end.
10. **Emerging standards / fact-check framing** ("SGP.32: The Facts Behind the
    Headlines") — start with what people are saying, then correct the record; never
    dismissive of excitement, channel it toward accuracy.

## Writing Principles Distilled From These 10 Pieces

1. Lead with the problem, not the product.
2. Earn the positioning — expertise shows in explanation quality, not self-promotion.
3. Be the trusted advisor — explain both sides fairly, or provide a framework.
4. Ground future-looking content in evidence.
5. Make every paragraph independently useful (structure for scanning/snippets).
6. Match technical depth to the audience.
7. Use narrative for healthcare and event content.
8. Never oversell a new standard or technology.
