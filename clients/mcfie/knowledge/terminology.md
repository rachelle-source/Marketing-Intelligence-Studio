# McFie Insurance — Terminology

_Source: `mcfie-content-hub` skill (`references/core-concepts.md`)._

## Participating Whole Life Insurance

A policy issued by a mutual insurance company that provides a guaranteed death benefit,
builds guaranteed cash value each year, pays (non-guaranteed but historically consistent)
dividends, and is owned as a **financial asset — not an investment**.

McFie recommends policies that are: written with a mutual (not stock) company, participating,
engineered for maximum cash value (high PUA rider, minimized base), not a Modified Endowment
Contract, and not bundled with unnecessary term riders.

**First-year cash value benchmark:** 60–70% of the first-year premium.

## Policy Loan

A **collateral assignment** of a portion of paid-up insurance to the insurance company; the
company lends from its general fund using the paid-up insurance as collateral.

- Not a withdrawal — cash value continues compounding as if the loan doesn't exist
- Not regulated by government agencies
- Outstanding loans reduce the death benefit if unpaid at death
- A lapsed policy with an outstanding loan is treated as a withdrawal (may trigger taxes)
- **Never call it a "bank loan"** or say you're "borrowing your own money"

## Paid-Up Insurance Rider (PUA Rider)

Optional rider: extra premium converts immediately to paid-up insurance. The engine of high
cash-value policies. Commissions on PUA riders are lower than base-policy commissions —
which is why some agents don't design for it.

## Modified Endowment Contract (MEC)

A policy that fails the IRS 7-pay test. Subject to LIFO taxation on withdrawals and a 10%
penalty before age 59½. Most clients want to avoid MEC status; rare exceptions exist (estate
planning, single-premium scenarios).

## Mutual vs. Stock Insurance Company

- **Mutual** — owned by policyholders, profits shared as dividends. McFie's recommendation
  for participating whole life.
- **Stock** — owned by shareholders.

## Infinite Banking Concept (IBC)

Created by Nelson Nash (*Becoming Your Own Banker*); uses whole life insurance as a personal
banking system. Certified practitioners are trained through the Nelson Nash Institute. McFie
is not affiliated with IBC LLC or the Nelson Nash Institute, but is knowledgeable about it.

## Term Life Insurance

Death benefit for a defined period (5/10/15/20/30 years), no cash value. McFie recommends
term only when whole life isn't yet affordable or practical.

## IUL / VUL / UL (Universal Life variants)

Not recommended by McFie for most clients — these shift risk to the policyholder and lack
the guarantees of participating whole life.

## Additional terminology (from `insurance-binder-builder` skill document, provided directly by user, 2026-08-07)

- **Base Premium** — the required premium to keep a policy in force.
- **Cash Surrender Value** — the amount available if a policy is canceled outright.
- **Non-Direct Recognition** — the policy loan interest rate is set equal to or close to
  the dividend rate, so borrowing has minimal net drag on growth.
- **Direct Recognition** — the dividend rate may be reduced on the borrowed portion of
  cash value while a loan is outstanding.
- **High Cash Value Design** — maximizes the Paid-Up Additions rider and minimizes the
  base death benefit relative to premium, for faster cash value accumulation (efficient
  for banking-style strategies). Contrast with **Traditional Design** — higher death
  benefit, lower premium relative to coverage, slower cash value growth, protection-
  focused.
- **Timeline expectations** — years 1-7: foundation-building, fees/mortality charges
  highest; years 7-15: cash value typically surpasses total premiums paid, dividends grow
  more significant; years 15+: mature policy, potential to reduce/eliminate premium
  payments, full banking functionality available.
