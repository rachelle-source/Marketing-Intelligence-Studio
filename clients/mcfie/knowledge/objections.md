# McFie Insurance — Objections & Reframes

_Source: `mcfie-content-hub` skill (`references/phrases-to-avoid.md`, `references/voice-tom.md`)._

These are documented "wrong framing → right framing" pairs from the brand's own rules —
use them to handle objections without ever using the wrong-side language.

## "Isn't whole life insurance just a bad investment?"

- Wrong: "Whole life insurance is a great investment."
- Right: "Whole life insurance is a financial asset." (The U.S. Supreme Court ruled it is
  an asset; several states prohibit calling it an investment.)

## "What kind of returns can I expect?"

- Wrong: "Expect 4–6% guaranteed growth."
- Right: "Whole life offers contractually guaranteed growth — exact rates depend on your
  policy and company."

## "Can this make me rich / financially free?"

- Wrong: "Infinite Banking can make you wealthy."
- Right: "Infinite Banking can help you keep more of the money you make and build financial
  stability over time."

## "Isn't a policy loan basically borrowing your own money from a 'bank' you built?"

- Never call a policy a "bank" (regulatory scrutiny). Use "policy," "participating whole
  life policy," or "financial tool."
- Never call a policy loan a "bank loan" or say you're "borrowing your own money" — the
  insurance company lends its own money, using your paid-up insurance as collateral.

## "[Financial personality] says whole life is a rip-off."

- Wrong: "Dave Ramsey is wrong about life insurance."
- Right: "The advice to 'buy term and invest the difference' doesn't account for..."
  (address the idea, never the person by name).

## "Why didn't McFie join Bank on Yourself if the underlying asset is the same?"

Tom's voice — addressed directly and without bitterness: joining required giving up the
right to choose which insurance company a client's application is submitted to. McFie
believes the responsibility to know what different companies offer, and the art of finding
the right fit for the client, is too important to give up.

## "Is Infinite Banking a scam?"

Ben's voice — addressed head-on: IBC got a bad reputation because some agents wrote
low-PUA policies (commissions are lower on PUA riders, so some agents skip them) and called
them "Infinite Banking Policies" when they weren't properly designed. The concept still
works when the policy is designed correctly.

## Hard rules

- Never promise specific returns or guarantee outcomes.
- Never disparage named financial personalities — address ideas, not people.
- Never recommend IUL/VUL/UL as "good alternatives" — acknowledge they exist, explain
  tradeoffs honestly.
