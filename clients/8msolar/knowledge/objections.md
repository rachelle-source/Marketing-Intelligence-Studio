# 8MSolar — Objections & Misconceptions

## Style for handling objections/misconceptions

Name the misconception directly as a declarative statement, not a hedge ("Solar Panels
Lead to Roof Harm" — not "Some people worry that..."). State it, then refute it with a
fact or data point. This reads as more honest and is more shareable than a hedged
question format.

## Known misconception categories to address

- Solar panels damage your roof
- Solar only works in sunny conditions (cited: ~35% of survey respondents believe this)
- Solar doesn't work on cloudy days / in winter / during a power outage
- Solar panels aren't worth it financially
- You can't get solar approved by your HOA

## Solar fraud / bad actors

Discussing dishonest solar dealers, door-to-door scams, and inflated savings claims is a
genuine service to readers — it protects them, and makes 8MSolar look trustworthy by
contrast without 8MSolar having to say so directly. This is the highest-trust editorial
move available: consumer protection over pure sales messaging.

## DIY vs. professional installation (a genuinely balanced objection)

- Acknowledge DIY's real appeal honestly: potential cost savings, sense of
  accomplishment. Don't dismiss it.
- Then present the risks in order of severity: safety first, then warranty invalidation
  (DIY installs can void manufacturer warranties — a serious risk on a 25-year
  investment), then permitting complexity.
- Let the reader reach the "professional installation is recommended" conclusion
  themselves rather than asserting it upfront — this is what makes the conclusion feel
  earned rather than self-serving.

## Cost objection reframe

- Never dodge the price question.
- Reframe toward long-term value: volume purchasing power, 25-year warranties, and
  in-house engineering reduce risk over the system's life even if upfront price is
  comparable to or higher than a smaller competitor.

## Hard rules

- Never fabricate a statistic to strengthen an objection response.
- Acknowledge genuine downsides (upfront cost, weather dependence, space requirements)
  without undermining the overall pro-solar message — over-hedging on every point reads
  as evasive, not honest.
