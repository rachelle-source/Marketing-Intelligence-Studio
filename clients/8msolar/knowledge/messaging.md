# 8MSolar — Messaging

_Source: `8msolar-content-hub` skill document, provided directly by user, 2026-08-07._

## Content Mission

Inspire energy independence through clear, authoritative, homeowner-focused articles that
rank on Google.

## The 8MSolar Voice

Think of a knowledgeable, friendly energy professional explaining solar to a homeowner
across a kitchen table:

- **Confident** — knows the material and says so clearly
- **Plain-spoken** — no jargon, no corporate speak
- **Empathetic** — understands the reader has real concerns about cost, reliability, and
  making the right decision
- **Encouraging** — solar is a positive step, and the reader should feel that

Conversational but professional. Never stuffy. Never dumbed-down.

## Editorial Standards (every article must pass)

| Standard | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Empathetic | Does the reader feel validated? |
| Relatable | Does this connect with their real life? |
| Authoritative | Is it well-researched with reputable sources? |
| Clear | Is it easy to understand? |
| Life-Changing | Does it move the reader toward action? |

## Reading Level & Style Rules

- Target: 8th-10th grade. Short sentences mixed with longer ones for rhythm.
- Active voice: "Solar panels reduce your bill," not "Your bill is reduced by solar panels."
- Define jargon before using it.
- Use "you"/"your" for the reader, "we" when speaking as 8MSolar.
- No Oxford comma required, but be consistent within an article.

## Word Choice Rules

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| yearn to understand | want to understand |
| unearth new insights | share new insights |
| illuminate the pivotal role | discuss the important role |
| embark on a journey | get started |
| leverage | use |
| delve into | look at / explore |
| holistic approach | complete approach |
| robust | strong / reliable |
| innovative solutions | new solutions / better solutions |
| paradigm shift | major change |
| cutting-edge | advanced / latest |
| seamlessly | smoothly / easily |
| transformative | significant / life-changing |

General rule: if a word sounds like it came from a marketing brochure or a TED Talk, cut it.

## Tone by Situation

- **Discussing costs**: be direct and honest, give real numbers, acknowledge the upfront
  investment is significant, then explain why it's worth it with data.
- **Discussing concerns/downsides**: don't dodge — acknowledge honestly ("Solar does
  require an upfront investment...") then address with facts.
- **Discussing NC/VA specifically**: always connect to local context — Duke Energy, NC
  incentives, local climate (humidity, hurricane season, sun hours), regional rates.
- **Concluding**: warm and action-oriented — the reader CAN do this, and 8MSolar is here
  to help.

## What to Avoid

- Wishy-washy statements — take a position, mention objections briefly, then move on
- Repetition — say it once, clearly
- Robotic rhythm — vary paragraph length
- Over-hedging ("Solar may or may not be right for you depending on various factors...")
- Excessive promotion — 8MSolar mentioned naturally in intro/conclusion only
- Fabricated statistics — never invent data
